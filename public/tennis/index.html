Data, definitions & limitations

Source: Jeff Sackmann / Tennis Abstract, preserved by the Aneeshers archive. A fixed historical snapshot, not a live ATP feed. View source archive ↗

Completed singles at Grand Slams, Masters, main-tour and Tour Finals events. Team events, Next Gen, retirements, defaults and walkovers are excluded. Tokyo Olympics is included because this archive classifies it as a main-tour event. 2020 had a disrupted calendar. The archive has not been independently audited against every official result.

Win rate = wins / appearances. Serve and return percentages use sums of points, not averages of match percentages. Rankings are recorded at tournament start. Dates refer to tournament start, not the exact match day. Missing or inconsistent point statistics are excluded from rate denominators; results remain in match counts. The minimum-match control sets leaderboard eligibility (20 by default). Rankings are descriptive; a small sample can produce extreme rates.

These are descriptive comparisons, not causal estimates or match predictions. Players face different opponents and schedules. Source data and derived extracts: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, attribution to Jeff Sackmann / Tennis Abstract; non-commercial use.

A QUICK START

Explore with a question.

  1. Choose your scope. Season, surface and tournament filter every view.
  2. Compare fairly. Set a minimum sample size before ranking players. Use the same surface and period.
  3. Go deeper. Click a player in a table or a scatter point to open their profile. Compare two players or inspect individual matches.
  4. Take the analysis with you. Copy a filtered view link, or export its player-match rows as CSV.

Each match has two player rows. CSV exports preserve that grain; count distinct MatchID values to count matches. Missing statistics stay blank. No prediction or live scores.
